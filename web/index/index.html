1. Term list

The terms and names the index covers, one per line, with other forms to search for in parentheses: artificial intelligence (AI, machine learning). Names go under a line Names:, written Last, First; the proofs are searched for “First Last”. A line indented under an entry is a subentry, or a cross-reference written see … or see also …; [ambiguous] after an entry asks for its hits to be judged. A list from book-index works as it is.

Propose terms from the manuscript

A model reads the manuscript passage by passage and proposes entries: people (surname first), places, organizations, works and concepts, with other forms they take. Each proposal quotes its passage, and is left out when the quotation is not there. Bibliographies are left out, by the paragraph labels the Formatter keeps in this browser for the same file or else by their headings; footnotes and endnotes are not read. You then accept, drop, merge, rename or add.

2. The proofs

Choose the typeset book as a PDF with selectable text. It is read in this browser and every term of the list is found on its pages, with no model, as book-index finds them. The optional helpers below then use a model where that saves you hand work.

Choose the proofs

Leave this empty to use the page numbers stored in the PDF, or to count from its first page if it has none. Pages before it are skipped.

When on, “US” finds “US” but not “us”, and “Bush” the name but not the shrub.

Pages the index does not cover

Front matter, sections of notes, bibliographies and an index already in the proofs. Type PDF page numbers, or let a model look at the first lines of each page and propose the ranges, each with the heading it saw on its first page; you confirm each one. A range that begins in the middle of a page keeps the text above its heading.

Ambiguous terms

Entries whose hits may not all mean the entry: marked by you, a capitalized term found in lower case, a short abbreviation, a word another entry also searches for, a surname shared by people in the list, hits inside another entry’s longer hit. A model can read each hit with the words around it and propose keeping or dropping it, with a reason. Nothing is dropped until you apply it, and you can change each one.

Subentries

A page goes to a subentry when the subentry’s own words are on it. A model can read the other hits of an entry with subentries and propose the subentry each belongs to; you apply them entry by entry.

3. The index

Set the index as your press asks. Loading the rule sheet you made with the Formatter sets these from the guidelines when they say.

Index style

The manuscript and the PDF are opened in this browser and are not uploaded. When you choose “Propose terms”, passages of the manuscript’s text (its paragraphs, without bibliographies, footnotes or endnotes) are sent through the CUNY AI Lab Gateway to open-weight models under no-retention terms; when you choose to find the pages the index does not cover, the first lines and short lines of each page of the PDF are sent the same way; when you choose to judge hits or sort them into subentries, the entry, its search forms, its subentries and the words around each of its hits are sent the same way. Nothing else leaves this browser, and nothing is stored on the server. This browser keeps the term list, the settings, the pages left out, the PDF’s and the manuscript’s file names and the decisions you and the models made (keep or drop, which subentry), but not the files or text from them, so that a reload or a new sign-in loses nothing. Downloading does not delete them. They are deleted 24 hours after your last change, also while this page stays open, and at once when you choose Start over or Delete saved work now.

This browser could not keep a copy of your work. Download the term list before you close the page.
