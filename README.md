# Manuscript Desk

A CUNY AI Lab tool suite that prepares a finished manuscript for a publisher's or journal's submission guidelines. It reads the guidelines into a rule sheet that the author reviews, applies the mechanical changes as Word tracked changes, and lists what only the author can supply. It has two tools: the Formatter and the Index Builder.

It is for CUNY AI Lab members, behind CUNY Login, at `https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/manuscript/`. It is a beta under test: the author checks every result before submitting it.

## Privacy

Your files are opened in the browser and are not uploaded. To read the guidelines and to label the manuscript's paragraphs, passages of their text are sent through the CUNY AI Lab Gateway to open-weight models under no-retention terms; when the rule sheet asks for running titles, the chapter titles are sent the same way; and in step 6, unless the author unticks the decide step, the title block, abstract, keywords, headings and the first pages of each chapter's text, the notes on contributors and the front matter (title page and contents list) are sent the same way, and then the queries found there (each with its paragraph, and, when the model asks for them, other paragraphs of the same chapter), the layout settings of the rule sheet with the sentences they came from, and the rules of the guidelines no check verified, so that a model can sort them. The manuscript is formatted in the browser. Nothing is stored on the server. The browser keeps the guidelines' text, the rule sheet, the paragraph labels and the author's corrections to them (in IndexedDB, with the manuscript's file name and hash and the model's answers per passage, which hold roles only), but not the manuscript or its text, so that a reload or a new sign-in loses nothing. Downloading the files does not delete them, so the author can look at the result in Word and come back to correct labels. They are deleted together 24 hours after the last change, also while the page stays open (a timer, and a check whenever the page is shown again or regains focus, `web/shared/store.js`), and at once on Start over or "Delete saved work now"; after the latter nothing more is kept while that page stays open.

The Index Builder works the same way: the manuscript and the proofs' PDF are opened in the browser and are not uploaded. Only what a model helper the author chooses needs is sent through the Gateway: passages of the manuscript's text (without bibliographies, footnotes or endnotes) to propose terms; the first lines and short lines of each PDF page to find the pages the index does not cover; an entry, its search forms, its subentries and the words around each of its hits to judge or sort them. It keeps its own saved work, without text from the files, by the same 24-hour rule (see The Index Builder page).

## The Formatter page

`/manuscript/format/` takes a scholar from guidelines to finished files in six steps:

1. What is being submitted: an article, a monograph, an edited collection or a dissertation or thesis.
2. The guidelines: PDF or Word files, or pasted text, read in the browser. For a dissertation, the CUNY Graduate Center's requirements are built in and offered first (`lib/presets/`): a rule sheet written by hand from the Mina Rees Library's published pages, each rule quoting its page with the address and the day it was read, used with no model call; another institution's requirements are uploaded as usual. `npm run check-preset` (needs the network, so not part of `npm run check`) fetches the pages again and reports any quotation no longer found.
3. The rule sheet read out of them (`POST /manuscript/api/rules`, each part read once per group of categories; then each part is asked once, through `POST /manuscript/api/settings`, about the settings that reading did not fill, with the sentence that states each, and about the sentences of the part that read as requirements and that no reading quoted, a short list item counting as one when the sentence introducing its list asks something, and shown with that sentence), each rule with the sentence it came from, to edit, switch off, add to, download or load. A call the Gateway refuses for its rate limit is asked again after a pause, a few times.
4. The manuscript: one `.docx`, read in the browser (`lib/docx/read.js`) and held in the page's memory only, with its size in words and paragraphs.
5. What each paragraph is: "Read the manuscript" labels the paragraphs through `POST /manuscript/api/roles`, stating the number of model calls first, with progress and Stop. For a monograph or a dissertation, a pass in the browser then reads the file's own structure (`lib/roles/structure.js`, from what `lib/docx/read.js` gives each paragraph: its style name, its outline level, whether it starts a new page, its level in the file's contents list): a heading the model read as a section heading becomes a chapter's title block when the file marks it as a top-level division (two signs: its outline level, its contents level or a "Chapter 3" label, and a new page, its contents level or a label), a top-level heading that names front or back matter heads that part, and the chapter's headings are levelled again under its title; the model's label stands wherever the file says nothing, and the page says how many chapter titles it found this way. The review shows, by chapter (with the front and back matter, such as a contents list or notes on contributors, as groups of their own), the paragraphs that are not body text or bibliography entries (those the model could not label first), each with an excerpt, its label in a menu the scholar can change and its neighbours on demand; a filter shows every paragraph or one kind, with counts. The labels are kept in the browser by a hash of the file, with the corrections; after a reload or a sign-in the scholar chooses the same file again and nothing is labelled again (a stopped run resumes from the passages already answered), while another file is labelled from the start.
6. Format and download. By default the page runs the decide step (`lib/decide/`, below), after saying how long it takes (about so many minutes and model calls); unticked, it formats only, as before: the formatter, the To-do checks, the comments, the To-dos list and the companion documents run in the browser (`web/format/output-state.js`), running titles are asked of `POST /manuscript/api/running-titles` only when the rule sheet calls for them, and every file offered has passed the structural validator.

   **The decide step** does the work the guidelines settle and hands the author only the real decisions. It runs the pipeline and the reading check (each chapter's opening sent to `POST /manuscript/api/reading` for the queries an editor would make; a query is kept only when the words it quotes are in the paragraph it names), then:
   - **does by itself, in code** (`lib/decide/mechanical.js`), as declared tracked changes that the formatter makes after its own (`formatDocx`'s `edits`): the typed contents list replaced by a contents field Word builds from the chapter titles (its result typed in, so it shows before Word updates it); a repeated-author dash in a bibliography written as the name from the entry above, when a rule asks for full names; a title or heading typed in capitals retyped in title case, when a rule asks for title case; a passage typed twice in a row removed once; a stray letter on a line of its own removed; a list of illustrations added after the contents from the figures' numbers and captions, when a rule asks for one. The formatter already restarts notes per chapter, numbers headings, sets figure markers by chapter and rejoins quotations pasted one line per paragraph where the sheet asks; the decide step flags the last once per chapter.
   - **applies defaults where the guidelines are silent**, for books and articles, never for a dissertation (which keeps its built-in set): 1.5 line spacing, page numbers at the bottom centre, 2.54 cm margins, chapter labels (books only), a 1 cm hanging indent for bibliographies, 12 pt Times New Roman. A guideline rule always wins; each default is listed once, in the summary comment and the page, under "Defaults we applied because the guidelines do not say". Three defaults settle a kind of finding: two authors' names joined two ways are left as typed for the copyeditor; a length given as "about" a number tolerates a third either way; web addresses in bibliographies stay in full.
   - **asks a model small units** (`lib/decide/units.js`, `loop.js`) through `POST /manuscript/api/decide`, which takes the unit's kind, whether reading is still open, and the unit's short conversation, and passes OpenAI-style `tools` to the Gateway (`server/decide-prompts.js`, `lib/decide/tools.js`; thinking off; the Worker keeps nothing between requests). One unit checks the layout settings against the sentences they came from and may switch off a misread one; one per chapter decides the queries that sit in running text (the copyeditor's, or a question), with at most two turns to read more paragraphs; the rules no check verified are triaged forty at a time (done, not applicable, the copyeditor's, a thing to hand in and of which kind, or a decision). A rate-limit refusal is waited out (up to six pauses); a reply that runs out of room is asked again as two halves; two units run at a time.
   - **judges every answer in code** (`lib/decide/policy.js`): no change to the words of a title, a heading, a person's name, an affiliation, an abstract, a keyword list or a reference, and no division of a title, except the declared mechanical cases above; a refused change becomes a question for the author (or, in running text, a query for the copyeditor) and is counted by kind. A finding leaves the author's list only when an applied operation resolves it, a stated default settles it, or it is a possible slip in running text, which goes to the copyeditor; the model cannot drop one, and a query on protected text is always a question.

   The author receives: the formatted manuscript with one summary comment at the top (what we did, the defaults, how many decisions, where the copyeditor's list is) and a comment at each real decision (questions on neighbouring lines of a title block, an abstract or the notes on contributors share one, and a check that repeats across a chapter, such as highlighted text, gives one comment there), with at most one line a chapter pointing to the copyeditor's list; a **Checklist** of only the decisions and the things to hand in (one row per kind of thing), with a count line for the rest; **What we did** (the changes by kind, the defaults, the settings not applied, the changes refused and why, the decisions left, and every rule of the guidelines with what became of it); **Queries for the copyeditor** (the possible slips, by chapter, and the guidelines' rules about the prose); and the abstracts, alt-text and running-titles documents when the sheet calls for them. The page lists "We made these changes", "You need to decide these N things", the defaults and the copyeditor's list. If the model service fails, every finding it was to sort stays a question, and the page says so.

Every file the page takes (the guidelines, a rule sheet, the manuscript) can be chosen with its button or dragged onto its box, which is marked while a file is over it. Both ways go through the same check and the same code (`web/shared/drop.js`): the wrong type, a file too large or several files where one is expected each get a plain message, and a file dropped anywhere else on the page is ignored with a short hint instead of opening in place of the page. The file inputs stay as they were, labelled and in the tab order. The Index Builder's file inputs work the same way, and `tests/drop.test.mjs` checks that every file input of both pages sits in a drop box and is wired to it.

The page's decisions are pure functions that load under Node (`web/format/app.js` for steps 1 to 3, `manuscript-state.js` for 4 and 5, `output-state.js` for 6) and are unit-tested; `manuscript-step.js` and `output-step.js` draw steps 4 to 6. Every text from the manuscript, the guidelines or a model is set with `textContent`, which `tests/html-sinks.test.mjs` enforces.

## The Index Builder page

`/manuscript/index/` builds a book's index in two stages that share one term list.

The term list is in two groups, shown and typed apart. **Names** are people: the index prints each surname first and the proofs are searched for it in the order the text uses ("Shelley, Mary" is searched as "Mary Shelley"). **Subjects** are everything else (concepts, places, organizations, events, titles of works), searched for as written and by the other forms in parentheses. The Names box takes people as they are said, one per line, and puts each surname first (`indexFormOfName` in `lib/index/terms.js`, which ends in the same `personHeading` the merge of proposals uses: honorifics left out, a lower-case particle after the forenames, as in "Beauvoir, Simone de", a capitalized one kept with the surname, as in "Du Bois, W. E. B.", a "Jr." or "Sr." after a comma); a name typed with a comma is kept as typed, and so are a single name, a name ending in a ruler's number in capitals ("Elizabeth I"), one led by a title such as Saint, Pope or Queen, one with "of", and the name of a body. The box shows the index form beside each line before it is added; one that is wrong ("Leonardo da Vinci", "Mao Zedong", a double Spanish surname) is corrected in the list after adding. Each entry has one button to move it to the other group: into Names a heading not yet surname first is put in index form, and out of Names a surname-first heading keeps the order the text uses as a form to search for.

On file the list is book-index's syntax (one term per line, other forms to search for in parentheses, names under a `Names:` line written `Last, First` and searched as "First Last"), with additions an old list never has: a `Terms:` line, an indented line under an entry for a subentry or for a `see` / `see also` cross-reference, and `[ambiguous]` after an entry (`lib/index/termlist.js`). A list made for book-index loads unchanged and gives the same index; a list with no `Names:` line is all subjects, and the page offers to move the subjects that read like a person written surname first to Names. The page loads, edits and downloads it, in that syntax; only accepted entries go into the index and the download.

1. **Propose terms** (optional, at submission): the manuscript `.docx` is read in the browser and cut into passages of about 9,000 characters of whole paragraphs, leaving out bibliographies (by the Formatter's paragraph labels for the same file when this browser keeps them, otherwise by headings such as "Bibliography" or "Notes" and the reference-like paragraphs after them); footnotes and endnotes are not read. Each passage is asked once through `POST /manuscript/api/index/terms` for the entries an indexer would make, each quoting the passage; a proposal whose quotation is not in its passage is dropped. Code merges the proposals of all passages (the same person named two ways, a short forename or a quoted nickname, singular and plural, a title with its article and without, a form that is another entry's variant, a surname given alone when one person bears it), puts a person's lower-case particle after the forenames and leaves out honorifics, counts each entry in the manuscript with the stage-2 matcher and drops what occurs only in the bibliography (`lib/index/terms.js`). The proposals are shown in the two groups, people under Names first, each group with its own counts, ranked (proposed in more passages, on more pages, more often), and proposals below thresholds the author sets are hidden until asked for, never dropped: the pages of the manuscript (stretches of about 2,500 characters) a person is on, under Names; under Subjects, the same for a concept and for the other names (places, organizations, works, events). By default concepts on one page only are hidden and every name is shown, the point chosen from the measured trade-off (`lib/index/rank.js`, `docs/eval/index-loop.md`); people follow the other names' threshold until the author sets theirs. The author accepts, drops, renames, merges, marks ambiguous, moves between the groups (an entry moved stays shown whatever the thresholds) and adds. A stopped or interrupted run resumes from the passages already answered.
2. **Find the terms in the proofs**: the PDF is read with MuPDF in a Web Worker (`web/index/index-worker.js`) and matched with book-index's own code (`lib/index/indexer.js`, `lib/index/extract.js`, copied unchanged), with its page numbering ("Book page 1 is PDF page", else the PDF's page labels) and its "Match capitalization". With no model call this is book-index. Three optional helpers each say how many calls they make, show progress and can be stopped; a failed call can be asked again on its own:
   - pages the index does not cover (`POST /manuscript/api/index/ranges`, `lib/index/matter.js`): the model sees each page's first lines and short lines, in windows of pages, and proposes front matter, notes, bibliographies and an existing index, each quoting the heading on its first page; a range whose heading is not on that page is dropped; the author ticks the ones to leave out, and a range that starts mid-page keeps the text above its heading. Page numbers can also be typed;
   - ambiguous hits (`POST /manuscript/api/index/judge`, task `ambiguous`, `lib/index/judge.js`): the entries the author marks, or those the matcher finds ambiguous (`lib/index/hits.js`: a capitalized term found in lower case, a short all-capitals abbreviation, a form another entry also searches for, a surname shared by people in the list, hits inside another entry's longer hit), or both, as the author chooses, each choice with its number of calls; each hit is judged with the words around it, keep or drop with a reason, and a drop counts only with evidence, words of that hit's own context that code finds there; the decisions are shown per entry with the evidence, applied only when the author applies them, and each can be reversed;
   - subentries (the same route, task `subentries`): a page goes to a subentry when the subentry's own words are on it; the model is asked page by page, with the words around each hit on the page, which subentry the page belongs to, applied per entry.
3. **The index**: indented or run-in, page ranges full, abbreviated as Chicago does or with at least two digits, letter-by-letter or word-by-word order, consecutive pages joined into ranges or not (`lib/index/ranges.js`, `lib/index/format.js`); loading a Formatter rule sheet sets these from its `index.layout`, `index.page-ranges` and `index.sorting` settings. It downloads as text, as a Word file in Word's "index 1" and "index 2" styles that has passed the structural validator, and as book-index's own `term: pages` list.

The page keeps its work in its own IndexedDB database (`manuscript-desk-index`): the term list (each entry with its group, and whether the author moved it; an entry saved without a group comes back under Names when the model called it a person and under Subjects otherwise) and the proposals without their quotations, the settings, the page ranges as numbers and kinds, the files' names and hashes, and the decisions (keep or drop, which subentry; not the model's reasons), never the files or text from them. It is deleted 24 hours after the last change, also while the page stays open, and at once on Start over or "Delete saved work now"; deleting it leaves the Formatter's work alone. The page's decisions are pure functions that load under Node (`web/index/state.js`, `web/index/terms-view.js`); `web/index/app.js` draws them. `tests/browser/index-smoke.mjs` takes the page through both stages and every helper with the API mocked, as `formatter-smoke.mjs` does for the Formatter, dropping the files on their boxes (`tests/browser/drop-files.mjs`), and goes through the two boxes, a move each way and the refused drops.

## How it runs

It is a Cloudflare Worker (`server/worker.js`) with static assets built into `dist/`. Every model call uses one model, `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` (the constant is in `lib/calls/model.js`): the pages offer no choice and send no model, and the Worker uses that one whatever a request says. `GET /manuscript/api/model` tells the pages whether the Gateway's catalog still lists it; when the catalog lists models but not this one, both pages say the tool is unavailable for now and every model route answers `503 model_unavailable` without calling anything, rather than falling back to another model. The measurement scripts call the Gateway with their own key and may name another model for a run; `MANUSCRIPT_DESK_MODEL=<id> npm run dev` does the same for the local dev server only. The Worker has no public address of its own: the portal (CAIL Doorway) handles sign-in and access, then forwards two signed identity assertions over a service binding. The Worker verifies both and calls models through the CAIL Gateway, which it reaches only through its `GATEWAY` service binding.

When the Worker answers that the session is missing or has ended (`authentication_required`, `session_invalid`), the page saves its work and goes to the launch path `/launch/manuscript` so sign-in restarts. It does that at most once per page, and only when `sessionStorage` can record it, so at most once a minute across reloads; otherwise it stays where it is and offers a "Sign in with CUNY" link (`web/shared/api.js`, as in the Bibliography Converter). An identity assertion that fails verification (`invalid_credential`) only shows the Worker's message: signing in again would not cure it. A session that stays invalid therefore cannot bounce the page between the tool and the launch path.

## Develop

Requires Node.js 22.

```bash
npm ci                          # CAIL packages are public on npm; no token needed
npm run vendor                  # copy MuPDF from node_modules into web/vendor/mupdf (git-ignored)
npm run check                   # vendor, syntax checks, all tests, then the build into dist/
npm test                        # tests only
npm run build                   # build dist/ (needs npm run vendor first)
npm run dev                     # build, then serve the pages and the Worker's routes at http://127.0.0.1:8787/manuscript/ (see below)
node scripts/verify-release.mjs # check wrangler.toml keeps the Worker private and bound to the Gateway (--deployed: see Release)
npx wrangler deploy --dry-run --outdir /tmp/cail-manuscript-dry   # offline bundle check
```

The tests stub every upstream and never call a model.

A browser smoke test of the built Formatter page, with the API mocked by request interception, lives in `tests/browser/` and is not part of `npm test` or `npm run check`. It goes through all six steps, choosing the invented manuscripts in `tests/fixtures/` (written by `node tests/fixtures/make-manuscripts.mjs`), and checks that every download is a valid Word file. Playwright is not a dependency; point the script at any copy of it:

```bash
npm run build
PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE=/path/to/node_modules/playwright/index.mjs node tests/browser/formatter-smoke.mjs
```

It uses Playwright's own Chromium if installed, otherwise the installed Google Chrome. `tests/workerd.test.mjs` runs the bundled Worker in real workerd through Miniflare.

Layout:

- `server/worker.js`: the Worker routes.
- `web/`: the pages. `web/vendor/mupdf/` is written by `npm run vendor`.
- `lib/`: the browser modules, testable in Node: `guidelines/` and `rules/` (the rule sheet), `docx/` (the manuscript reader, the package writer, the comments writer, a builder for new documents and the structural validator), `roles/` (paragraph roles), `format/` (the formatting stage), `checks/` (the To-do checks), `todos/` (the To-dos list and the companion documents) and `decide/` (the decide step).
- `scripts/`: build, vendoring, local test server, release check and the extraction, roles, formatting and To-dos measurements.
- `tests/`: Node tests; fixtures are text written for the tests, never real guidelines or manuscripts. `tests/fixtures/catalog-2026-11-09.json` is a copy of the public Gateway model catalog.

## Release

Releases go through the GitHub Actions workflow **Check and private release** (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) only, never from a local machine. It is the Bibliography Converter's release job with this Worker's names.

### Order of a first release

1. **This Worker, privately.** Merge to `main` and wait for the workflow's `check` job (`npm ci`, `npm run check`, `wrangler deploy --dry-run`). Then run the workflow by hand from `main` with the exact current 40-character commit of `main`, for example `gh workflow run ci.yml --repo CUNY-AI-Lab/manuscript-desk --ref main -f sha="$(git rev-parse origin/main)"` after a fetch. The `release` job runs in the `production` environment. It refuses a commit that is not the current `main` (before the build, before the deploy and after the readback), checks that `CAIL_IDENTITY_JWKS` verifies both audiences, `cail:manuscript` and `cail:gateway`, writes it to a private secrets file, deploys with `wrangler deploy --strict --tag <sha> --message "commit <sha>" --secrets-file …`, and runs `node scripts/verify-release.mjs --deployed`. The Worker `cail-manuscript` then exists with no public address: `workers_dev` and `preview_urls` are off and it has no route, so nothing reaches it yet.
2. **Then Doorway mounts it**, in `CUNY-AI-Lab/cail-tools-admission` (not changed here): a service binding to `cail-manuscript`, the routes under `/manuscript` (pages and `/manuscript/api`) forwarding both identity assertions, `cail:manuscript` and `cail:gateway`, for one person, and the launch path `/launch/manuscript`. Doorway's release checks that its bindings resolve, so it cannot go first.
3. **Then a signed-in member's acceptance run** (below). The release job has no CUNY session and proves only what Cloudflare reports.

To roll back, revert on `main` and release the new `main` commit: the job releases only the current `main`.

### Secrets of the `production` environment

Set in GitHub: repository `CUNY-AI-Lab/manuscript-desk`, Settings, Environments, `production`. Names only here; never put a value in the repository, an issue or a log. Give the environment required reviewers, so a release needs someone's approval.

- `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`: a Cloudflare API token for the CUNY AI Lab account that may edit Workers scripts there. The release deploys with it and reads the deployment back with it.
- `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`: the id of that Cloudflare account. It must be the account that holds Doorway (`cail-doorway`) and the Gateway (`cail-model-api`), because service bindings resolve only within one account.
- `CAIL_IDENTITY_JWKS`: the fleet's pinned identity verifier key set (public keys, as JSON), the same one Doorway's assertions are checked against and the other tools hold. The release refuses it unless it verifies both audiences, then installs it on the Worker as a secret. `--secrets-file` adds and replaces, never deletes: a secret removed from GitHub stays on the Worker until it is deleted there.

The job also uses `github.token` for the checks against `main`; nothing else is configured by hand. No Google Books key or other secret is used.

### Verify

- The release job's last lines: `wrangler.toml passes the release check.` and `Private Worker control-plane readback passed: commit <sha>, version <id>, 100% traffic.` The readback (`scripts/verify-release.mjs --deployed`) asserts, for `cail-manuscript`: one version at 100% of traffic tagged with the commit, workers.dev and previews off, the `GATEWAY` service binding to `cail-model-api`, the `CAIL_IDENTITY_JWKS` secret, the issuer, the assets and version-metadata bindings, and the runtime's compatibility date and flags. It reads Cloudflare's configuration only; it does not call the Worker.
- Once Doorway mounts it, signed in: `https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/manuscript/health` answers `status: "ready"` with the release's `version_id` and `tag` (the commit). It loads the verifier keys and reads the public model catalog through the `GATEWAY` binding, so it proves the Gateway path without spending anything. Signed out, `/manuscript/` goes to sign-in and `/manuscript/api/session` is refused.
- A member then takes the invented test files in `tests/fixtures/` (never a private manuscript) through all six steps of `https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/manuscript/format/`, opens the downloads in Word, and records the Doorway and Worker version ids with the outcome.
- The Gateway reports this tool's model calls as unattributed until Doorway names the tool in the Gateway assertion and the Gateway accepts its id (see `docs/eval/brill-loop.md`, "Where things stand"); the spend still counts against the person's allowance.

## Try it locally with real models

```bash
CAIL_API_KEY=sk-cail-... npm run dev
```

This builds `dist/` and serves it with the Worker's real routes at `http://127.0.0.1:8787/manuscript/format/` and `http://127.0.0.1:8787/manuscript/index/` (`PORT` sets another port). The Formatter works as in production, through all six steps: the allowance loads, and reading the guidelines, labelling the manuscript and proposing running titles make real model calls through the public CAIL Gateway (a book of 1,460 paragraphs took 85 labelling calls and under five minutes). Every call is charged to the key in `CAIL_API_KEY`, so it spends the key owner's own model allowance; the allowance shown is that key's.

Sign-in is simulated. The server makes a throwaway signing key when it starts and signs both identity assertions for one fixed local person on every request, so there is no CUNY Login; it drops any credential the browser sends and puts the key on the Gateway calls instead. It listens on 127.0.0.1 only, answers only requests addressed to `localhost` or `127.0.0.1` on its port, and accepts a POST only from its own pages. It logs one line per API request (method, path, status, milliseconds), never a body, and never prints the key. Without `CAIL_API_KEY` the pages are served but model calls fail.

This mode exists only in the dev script (`scripts/dev.mjs`, `scripts/dev-server.mjs`). The deployed Worker is unchanged: it has no key path and accepts no local origin.

## Measuring rule extraction

`scripts/generality.mjs` measures how well the rule sheet comes out of real publishers' guidelines with real models. It runs the product's own reading, prompt, model controls and extraction code, calls the Gateway at `https://tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu/v1/chat/completions` directly, and checks each sheet against rules we have written down as expected.

```bash
CAIL_API_KEY=... npm run generality -- --models deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017,other-model [--only <folder>] [--all [--doc-type article]] [--repeat <n>] [--save <folder>]
```

`--repeat <n>` reads each case n times and adds, per case, a table of the rule sheet's settings: in how many runs each was filled and with which values (a text value, such as a font name or a marker's example, only as a label), and how many were filled in every run. `--save <folder>` writes each rule sheet as JSON to that folder, which must be outside the repository, since a sheet quotes the guidelines. `--record <folder>` writes every model answer, with the request it answered, to that folder (outside the repository too), and `--replay <folder>` scores again from such a recording without calling the Gateway.

Every call is charged to the key in `CAIL_API_KEY`, so a run spends your own model budget; the script reports the tokens used and their cost from the public model catalog. The key is read only from that variable and never printed.

Real guidelines stay outside the repository. The script reads the folder named by `MANUSCRIPT_DESK_GUIDELINES` (default `../manuscript-desk-guidelines`, next to this checkout), with one subfolder per publisher holding its guideline files (PDF, `.docx`, `.html`, `.txt`), possibly in further subfolders. A subfolder with an `expected.json` is a scored case:

```json
{
  "docType": "monograph",
  "files": ["author-guide.pdf", "style/finalizing.docx"],
  "rules": [{ "name": "alt texts for all images", "any": ["ALT-texts for all images"] }]
}
```

`files` (paths relative to that subfolder) lists the documents read together as one set of guidelines; without it, every supported file directly in the subfolder is used. A rule counts as found when any of its `any` strings occurs, ignoring case, quote style and dashes, in an extracted rule's quotation or statement. A model passes when it finds at least 90 percent of all expected rules and at least 80 percent in every case; the script exits non-zero unless every model passes.

By default only scored cases run. `--all` also runs every other supported file as its own unscored case (a monograph unless `--doc-type` says otherwise), reported with counts only; `--only <folder>` runs one subfolder. Output goes to stdout: per model, one row per case with rules kept, left out, expected found, failed calls, calls made and seconds, then totals, cost, the number of settings questions and of calls the Gateway refused, PASS or FAIL, the names of missed expected rules and the settings each case's questions filled. It never prints guideline text, quotations or rule statements, and writes nothing to disk unless `--save` or `--record` is given.

## Measuring paragraph roles

`scripts/roles-eval.mjs` measures how well the paragraphs of a real manuscript are labelled with their roles (title block, heading, block quotation, verse, bibliography entry, body and so on), against roles set by hand. It runs the product's own `.docx` reader (`lib/docx/read.js`), passages and labelling run (`lib/roles/`) and the payload `POST /manuscript/api/roles` sends, through the public Gateway with the key in `CAIL_API_KEY`:

```bash
CAIL_API_KEY=... npm run roles-eval -- --chapters dev [--model <id>] [--case <folder>] [--save <file>]
```

A case is a folder under `manuscripts/` in `MANUSCRIPT_DESK_GUIDELINES` with `source.docx` and `gold-roles.json`; the gold may list spans of front and back matter outside the chapters (`matter`), which `--chapters dev` and `all` measure and report apart. The script prints accuracies, counts and confusion tallies only, never manuscript text. It caches every model answer in the case's `work/roles-answers.json` (`--cache <file>` keeps a run's answers in another file, for a fresh run), so a re-score pays for nothing it asked before, and `--save <file>` writes the roles it labelled. For a monograph or a dissertation (`--doc-type`, or the gold's `docType`) it scores the roles as the model gave them and again after the chapter-title pass; a gold may give `scope`, a list of roles whose every paragraph it lists, so that a paragraph it does not list predicted with one of them counts as an error. The measured runs, the held-out result and the remaining confusions are in [`docs/eval/brill-loop.md`](docs/eval/brill-loop.md).

## Measuring formatting

`lib/format/format.js` turns a manuscript's `.docx`, its paragraph roles and a rule sheet into a new `.docx` in which structure is carried by named paragraph styles instead of direct formatting: one style per role (with a no-indent variant for the first paragraph after a heading or a quotation, and separate styles for a title block's title, subtitle, author and affiliation), empty spacer paragraphs removed, each chapter its own Word section when notes restart in every chapter, heading numbers through Word numbering and figure placement markers when the sheet asks for them. It keeps runs, note references, links, images, tables, fields and the character formatting that carries meaning, and declares every change to the text with the words it removed and added and the paragraphs it was made at. It also makes the tidy-ups that are right for any publisher: in a chapter's title block and a book's title page, spaces and line breaks at the ends of a line, quotation marks that open a title and close its last subtitle, a colon ending a title before its subtitle, a final full stop and brackets around a whole affiliation line go, a subtitle split from its title takes a capital, a subtitle broken over two lines is joined, an "Edited by" line is set as a label and an editor's "Name, Institution" line is split; headings lose spaces and line breaks at their ends; a heading repeated in every chapter (the bibliography's) typed with different capitals is made like the variant that follows the sheet's heading case, or like most of them; and a typed copy of a note's number next to its automatic mark goes. Latin text set by direct formatting in a font other than the body's takes the body font (symbol and code fonts, and the fonts of East Asian, complex-script and other scripts, stay), in the text, the notes, the headers and the footers, and the document defaults take the body font too. Sections the formatter adds at chapter starts show the page number on their first page, and a section whose page size and margins differ from those of most of the text takes theirs (a landscape section keeps its own); with a margins rule every section takes the rule's. With `layout.page-numbers` set, every section shows the page number where it says, replacing headers and footers that hold nothing else; with `layout.page-number-distance`, every section's header and footer stand at least that far from the edge of the page (a section where that leaves less than a quarter of an inch of margin beyond them, and a page number in a text box fixed on the page, which the distance does not move, are listed for the author); with `references.hanging-indent`, the Bibliography style takes that hanging indent; with `chapters.label` set, each numbered chapter without a label gets one in the form of the example; with `layout.spacing` and `layout.font`, the body takes them. What the sheet leaves unset (line spacing, page numbers, margins, font) stays as the manuscript has it, and the summary comment says so. The changes to words (a title split at its colon, a rewritten figure marker, an added caption line, the tidy-ups above) are tracked insertions and deletions by "CAIL Manuscript Desk"; style and layout changes are direct. `lib/docx/validate.js` checks the structure of the file it writes, its comments and its tracked changes.

`scripts/format-eval.mjs` measures it against a formatting job done by hand:

```bash
npm run format-eval -- [--predicted | --roles <file>] [--sheet hand|extracted|<file>] [--chapters dev|holdout|all] [--score <file>] [--pdf]
```

`--roles` takes the roles from a file (as the Formatter page keeps them), and `--score` measures a formatted file made elsewhere, such as a download from the page: the script formats the source with the same roles and sheet, checks that the file has the same paragraphs, text and styles, and measures that file.

A case is a folder under `manuscripts/` in `MANUSCRIPT_DESK_GUIDELINES` with `source.docx`, `gold-roles.json` and the hand-formatted `.docx` in `reference/`. The script formats the source with the gold roles (or, with `--predicted`, roles from the labelling stage) and a rule sheet (`hand` is a short sheet written for the case, `extracted` the product's own extraction from the publisher's guidelines), then prints counts only: paragraphs matched to the reference by their text, style agreement through the committed map in `docs/eval/brill-style-map.json`, sections, words (any change no declaration explains is a failure, and rejecting every tracked change must give back the source's words), the elements carried over, and the validator's result. The output and any model answer are written to the case's `work/` folder and model answers are reused from there, so nothing is paid for twice; model calls, made only when a cache is missing, use the key in `CAIL_API_KEY`. With `--pdf` it also converts the output with LibreOffice, if installed, to see that it opens. The measured runs are in [`docs/eval/brill-loop.md`](docs/eval/brill-loop.md).

## Measuring how the file looks

Style names agreeing with a hand-formatted file is not the same as the file looking like it. `scripts/render-eval.mjs` compares what a reader sees, without Word:

```bash
npm run render-eval -- <tool.docx> <reference.docx> [--case <folder>] [--pdf word|soffice --pdf-dir <folder>] [--pdfs <a.pdf>,<b.pdf>] [--json <file>]
```

`lib/docx/appearance.js` resolves each paragraph's effective formatting through the document defaults (with theme fonts), the style hierarchy, numbering and direct formatting: the font and size most of its text is set in, line spacing, space before and after, indents and alignment. The script matches the two files' paragraphs by their text (as `format-eval` does) and reports agreement per property, dev and holdout chapters apart when `--case` names a case (the source paragraph each paragraph came from gives its chapter), with the differences grouped into classes ("line: 2 -> 1.5 (reference Body Text): 328"); the same for the notes; each file's sections (page size, margins, note numbering, where the page number stands on a section's first page and on the others, and its font) and, by chapter, whether they agree; and the Latin text set in a font that is not the body's (East Asian and complex-script text takes the fonts made for it; symbol and code fonts are not counted), in the body, the notes and the headers and footers. With `--pdf` it exports both files with Microsoft Word (through AppleScript; Word on macOS writes only into folders it has been given access to, and otherwise stops on a "Grant File Access" dialog that a person must answer) or LibreOffice into a folder of its own and reports page counts and the characters set in each font of the PDFs (read with MuPDF); `--pdfs` reads PDFs made already. It prints counts, style names, font names and measurements only, never text.

## The To-dos

`lib/checks/` reads the paragraph model, the roles, the formatter's report and the rule sheet and returns findings, each with its kind, chapter, paragraph, rule and a message that says, in plain words, what is left to do. A check runs only when a rule in the sheet asks for what it checks (quotation marks kept out of chapter titles, abstracts and keywords, a running title's length, permissions, alt texts, and so on) or when it concerns a change the formatter made that the author must verify (a split title, joined quotation lines, added heading numbers); the checks that did not run are listed with what each needed. Two checks compare the front matter with the chapters: the manuscript's own contents list against the chapter titles and authors actually present (a chapter missing from it, a line that matches nothing, a title or name worded differently, authors in another order), and the notes on contributors against the chapters' authors (an author with no note, a note on no author, a name spelled differently, an affiliation the note does not give, a note far from the length asked). The findings become Word comments in the formatted file (`lib/docx/comments.js`, one per place, with a general comment at the top that counts what the pass changed) and the To-dos list, its own `.docx` (`lib/todos/todos.js`): every finding by chapter and kind, each naming its rule and quoting the guideline sentence; the requirements only the author can supply; and a summary of what was changed, flagged and skipped. Accessibility checks run on every formatted file whatever the rule sheet says (`lib/checks/accessibility.js`, reading the file through `lib/docx/accessibility.js`): pictures without alternative text, pictures that are probably images of text, skipped heading levels, tables without a marked header row, link text that says nothing or is a bare address, highlighting and coloured text, and the document's title and language. Each finding has a severity (must fix, should fix, check), names the WCAG principle and says where to fix it in Word and in Google Docs; they are comments and an Accessibility section of the To-dos list, tied to the sheet's accessibility rules when it has them. The formatter makes the two safe fixes, declared: the document title from the title page, and a table's first row marked as a header row when it is set apart as one. For a dissertation the checks also compare the order and presence of its parts and its title page with the sheet (`lib/checks/parts.js`; the title page is read by what each line says, wherever it stands, `titlePageParts` in `lib/format/titles.js`, with a logo or a table of names between its lines); nothing is moved or invented. Companion documents are made only when a rule asks for them (`lib/todos/companions.js`): abstracts and keywords per chapter, an alt-text template with every picture the accessibility check reads (also made when a picture has no alt text), running titles (proposed by a model through `POST /manuscript/api/running-titles`, kept only when they fit and use the title's own words) and a checklist of the sheet's requirements with a status for each.

`scripts/todos-eval.mjs` measures them against the comments of a job done by hand:

```bash
npm run todos-eval -- [--predicted | --roles <file>] [--sheet hand|extracted|<file>] [--chapters dev|holdout|all] [--running-titles] [--reading dev|holdout|all]
```

A case needs, besides the files above, a private answer key of the reference's comments in its `work/` folder (`comments-key.json`: chapter, paragraph, kind and class of each comment; `companions-key.json` for the companion documents). The script runs the product on the source and prints counts only: of the key's comments in each class (`structure`, `reading`, `human`), how many the tool produced (same kind, same chapter, within two paragraphs with text), the misses and the extra findings by kind, how the companion documents line up with the reference's, and the output's validity. The outputs go to `work/`. It makes no model call, except with `--running-titles` when the proposals are not cached, and with `--reading`, which runs the reading check on the dev, holdout or all chapters (answers cached in `work/reading-answers.json`), with the key in `CAIL_API_KEY`. The reading check's kept findings, with their quotations, go to the private `work/reading-findings-<tag>.json` for review; stdout gets their counts by place and kind. The measured runs are in [`docs/eval/brill-loop.md`](docs/eval/brill-loop.md).

## Measuring the decide step

`scripts/decide-eval.mjs` runs the Formatter page's own code on a private case: the pipeline alone (decide step unticked, reading check on) and the decide step twice, with the case's own labels and rule sheet from the page run, the reading check from its cache, and the decide step's units sent to the Gateway exactly as `POST /manuscript/api/decide` sends them.

```bash
CAIL_API_KEY=... npm run decide-eval -- [--case brill-dante] [--runs 2] [--no-render] [--tag <name>]
CAIL_API_KEY=... npm run decide-eval -- --dissertation <folder> [--runs 1]
```

Per run it prints counts only: comments in the file; of the hand job's questions, how many have a comment of the tool near (any comment, and question comments only); refusals by kind and text changes outside the declared mechanical kinds (must be 0); the operations against the hand job's; checklist rows; copyedit queries moved; undeclared word changes (0) and whether rejecting every change gives the source; the validator; appearance against the hand-formatted file through `render-eval` with LibreOffice headless; model calls, cost and minutes. Every answer is cached and every paid call entered in a ledger in the private folder's `work/decide/`; no call starts once the ledger reaches `DECIDE_CAP` dollars (default 8). The formatted files go to the case's `work/decide/<tag>/`. The measured runs are in [`docs/eval/decide-loop.md`](docs/eval/decide-loop.md).

## Measuring the Index Builder

`scripts/index-eval.mjs` measures the Index Builder against the index a published book actually has. A case is a folder under `index-books/` in `MANUSCRIPT_DESK_GUIDELINES` holding the book's PDF with its index pages taken out (`stripped.pdf`, with the printed page numbers as its page labels), the index parsed into entries, subentries, cross-references and pages (`gold.json`) and the pages an index does not cover, marked by hand (`ranges-hand.json`). These are made privately from copyrighted books and never committed.

```bash
CAIL_API_KEY=... npm run index-eval -- --book <folder> [--steps match,ranges,judge,subentries,terms] [--model <id>] [--book-index <path>] [--judge-runs <n>] [--judge-thinking on|off]
```

`match` gives the matcher the gold's headings as a term list (people in the names, by a model's sorting of the headings; other headings without their parenthetical qualifiers, searching also the part before a comma) and compares the pages found with the index's: precision and recall of pages, locators touched, entries exactly the same, and the disagreements sorted into kinds (a page with no search form on it, a page inside a range, an entry found nowhere; an extra page in front or back matter, next to a page the index gives, or elsewhere). It also runs book-index's own code from a checkout (`--book-index`, default `../book-index`) on the same PDF and list and says whether the two indexes are identical. `ranges`, `judge` and `subentries` measure the three helpers against the hand-marked ranges, the index's pages (and a sample of hits labelled by hand beforehand, when the case has one) and the index's subentries; `--judge-runs` asks the judge again, each run cached apart, and reports how far the runs agree and what two readings with only agreeing drops would give; subentries are scored per hit and per page as the page composes the index. `terms` runs stage 1 on the book's text (its PDF paragraphs outside the hand-marked ranges, a path of this script only) and scores the proposals against the index's headings, with round 1's matching and with a strict matching of people (a surname alone never matches a person named with forenames): recall by kind, the share of proposals that are index entries, duplicates left after merging, the surname-first form of names by the form of the index's heading (plain, particle, compound surname, title or rank, one name), the editor's filter at each threshold, and a sample of the other proposals written privately for judging by hand. It prints counts and rates only and caches every model answer in the case's `work/` folder. The measured runs are in [`docs/eval/index-loop.md`](docs/eval/index-loop.md).
